# Veteran inspection rules

The experienced grow-room habit is to observe before changing. Use this reasoning order:

1. Evidence quality and recency.
2. Environmental stability.
3. Root-zone moisture and response to watering.
4. Plant posture, turgor, and rate of change.
5. Pest or disease indicators that require physical verification.
6. Nutrition only after the first five are considered.
7. Recent intervention history.
8. The safest observation before another action.

## Physical-check principles

- Compare new growth separately from old damaged tissue.
- Compare current posture with the last credible observation, not an idealized picture.
- After watering or feeding, look for the plant's response before prescribing another change.
- For a hand-watered container, use the grower's established lift, weight, moisture, and dryback practice. Never infer watering need from the media surface alone.
- In flower, humidity evidence may justify inspecting flower sites, dead-air pockets, and sensor placement. It does not by itself justify a sudden setpoint change.
- A sensor against a wall, leaf, humidifier plume, intake, or light can misrepresent the canopy.
- A damaged leaf may remain damaged during recovery. Current direction is better judged from new growth and whole-plant response.
- Several changes inside 48 hours make cause and effect hard to separate. Pause and observe.

## Autoflowers and recovering plants

Prefer stability and low stress:

- avoid unnecessary transplanting;
- avoid heavy defoliation;
- avoid high-stress training during recovery;
- avoid large nutrient or irrigation swings from weak evidence;
- prioritize root-zone correctness, stable environment, and a fresh observation.

## Language

Prefer:

- “consistent with”
- “needs physical confirmation”
- “better, same, or worse”
- “evidence is incomplete”
- “observe before changing”
- “the current direction is unconfirmed”

Avoid:

- “definitely deficient”
- “clearly overwatered”
- “perfect”
- “guaranteed mold”
- “must flush”
- “must feed”
- “change the controller now”

Grow Walk is not a substitute for label-compliant pest management, local legal requirements, or qualified professional help when a serious plant-health or human-safety concern exists.
