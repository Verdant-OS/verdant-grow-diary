# Brief — Cannabis light-stress troubleshooting

- **Working title:** Cannabis Light Stress: How to Tell Light Burn, Bleaching, and Heat Stress Apart Before You React
- **Recommended slug:** `cannabis-light-stress-light-burn-bleaching-or-heat`
- **Primary intent:** Triage `light burn cannabis` and `cannabis light stress` without presenting a photo or one symptom as proof.
- **Secondary intents:** bleaching versus nutrient burn, top-canopy symptoms, heat-stress context, what to log after a light change.
- **Audience:** A grower seeing a new symptom near the canopy and deciding whether to change a fixture or investigate other causes.
- **Problem:** Similar-looking symptoms can arise from multiple changes; an aggressive one-cause answer can make the situation worse.
- **Unique Verdant angle:** A diary-first differential that asks what changed, when it changed, where symptoms appeared, and what the temperature/RH/light record says.

## Outline

1. Direct safety framing: a symptom pattern is not a diagnosis.
2. The evidence timeline: change date, location in canopy, progression, photos, and environmental record.
3. Light-intensity/bleaching pattern: what requires verification before acting.
4. Heat-stress context: why leaf temperature/air data and airflow context matter.
5. Nutrient/root-zone and water-status look-alikes: why not to change multiple levers.
6. Low-risk first response: pause escalation, check measurement/source, make one reversible change when justified.
7. What to log over 24 hours and three days.
8. FAQ and timeline CTA.

## Evidence and safety requirements

- Use evidence-led language such as “may be consistent with,” “compare,” and “needs context.”
- Cite horticultural/controlled-environment sources for light/heat physiology; never imply medical or pest-free certainty.
- Explicitly say that severe decline, pests, contamination, or unsafe electrical/heat conditions require appropriate local expertise.
- Avoid hard numeric remedies, universal feeding changes, and automatic Action Queue behavior.
